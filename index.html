I'm a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin, with a focus in applied machine learning. I am advised by Professors James Tunnell and Mia Markey. My research develops generative and multimodal AI for healthcare, with a primary application in improving skin cancer surgery. My work spans AI/ML, optical microscopy and spectroscopy, and medical image perception.

I also spent two summers as a computational imaging intern at Samsung Research America in the Mobile Processor Innovation Lab, working on generative AI and image quality assessment methods for computational photography. I received my M.S.E. from UT Austin in 2025 and my B.S. in bioengineering, with a minor in data science, from Rice University in 2023.

Outside the lab, I've led fundraising and advocacy for global health causes. I am also a rock climber and enjoy hiking, camping, and backpacking.

Research Highlights

Generative and multimodal AI for healthcare and imaging.

Multimodal AI Surgical Guidance for Skin Cancer
UT Austin, 2023–present

Develop multimodal AI methods for intraoperative guidance and clinical decision support in skin cancer surgery. This work adapts pathology foundation models to new imaging modalities where labeled data are scarce, and integrates optical microscopy and spectroscopy with generative AI for tissue characterization, tumor detection, and margin assessment, with an emphasis on interpretable, biologically grounded evaluation that can inform surgical decision-making.

Virtual Histopathology Staining
UT Austin, 2023–present

Develop generative imaging models that convert label-free microscopy of fresh, unprocessed skin excisions into multiplexed "virtual" histology. This work allows for tumor margins to be assessed intraoperatively without the time, cost, and laboratory infrastructure of conventional tissue processing.

Evaluation of Generative AI in Real-World Settings
UT Austin, 2023–present

Develop evaluation methods for generative imaging in healthcare applications. Standard image quality assessment metrics do not always align with preservation of underlying biology and human labels for clinical tasks are costly, noisy and often unavailable.

Computational Photography
Samsung Research America, Summers 2024 and 2025 Internships

Develop generative AI methods for mobile computational photography, with a focus on image restoration and enhancement in challenging conditions such as digital zoom and low light, using lightweight architectures designed for on-device deployment. This work also covers synthetic data generation that reflects real-world capture conditions, and automated image quality assessment for generative models using contrastive learning and reward modeling.

Automated Multi-Label Classification of Slide-Free Basal Cell Carcinoma Using a Pathology Foundation Model
Fadeel Sher Khan, Sanidhya D. Tripathi, Logan B. Parker, Matthew C. Fox, Jason S. Reichenberg, Katherine R. Sebastian, Mia K. Markey, James W. Tunnell
SPIE Photonics West, Advanced Diagnostic, Surgical Guidance, and Robotic Systems XXV, paper 14404-22, January 2027 (talk)
SPIE

Slide-free microscopy images tissue with minimal processing and costs less than standard histopathology, but automated diagnostic tools for it are limited by a lack of large training datasets. We adapt a pathology foundation model, pretrained on conventional histopathology images and clinical data, to slide-free fluorescence-imitating brightfield (FIBI) images of bulk excisions from 25 basal cell carcinoma patients, using parameter-efficient fine-tuning for multi-label classification. High validation accuracy suggests this approach can help address the dataset shortage in slide-free microscopy.

Context-Specific Image Quality Assessment for Virtual Histologic Staining: Checklist and Guideline
Fadeel Sher Khan, Mia K. Markey, Umberto E. Villa, Alan C. Bovik, Matthew C. Fox, Brett H. Keeling, Jason S. Reichenberg, James W. Tunnell
Journal of Medical Imaging, 13(S1): S11209, 2026
full text (PMC) publisher PubMed

Virtual staining turns optical images of tissue into histology-like images, but the metrics used to evaluate it often miss what matters for a particular biomedical use. We review current image quality assessment practice for virtual staining and propose a checklist and guideline built around a defined context of use, the imaging mechanism behind it, and evidence that links image quality to biological ground truth.

Conference
Graph-Based Biomarker Discovery and Interpretation for Alzheimer's Disease
Maryam Khalid*, Fadeel Sher Khan*, John I. Broussard, Arko Barman
* Equal first authors
IEEE International Conference on Healthcare Informatics (ICHI), 2026
pdf arXiv publisher

Blood biomarkers are a cheaper and more accessible route to early Alzheimer's diagnosis than imaging, but biomarkers interact in ways that ranking them one at a time misses. We introduce BRAIN, a framework that represents biomarkers as a graph to explain why different models pick different markers and to surface interacting subnetworks. On a public blood biomarker dataset it finds three subnetworks whose interactions differ between control and Alzheimer's groups.

Pixel Perfect: Relational Image Quality Assessment with Spatially-Aware Distortions
Fadeel Sher Khan, Long N. Le, Abhinau K. Venkataramanan, Seok-Jun Lee, Hamid R. Sheikh
arXiv preprint, 2026
pdf arXiv

Mean opinion scores are expensive to collect and say little about where or how an image is degraded. Pixel Perfect treats quality as a directional comparison against a reference: a network trained on self-supervised synthetic distortions predicts spatial maps of distortion type, strength, and direction, and a contrastively trained scorer outputs a relational quality score, with no human labels required.

Line-scan Fluorescence and Raman Confocal Microscope for Skin Cancer Surgical Guidance
Brian K. Lee, Logan B. Parker, Alex T. Whitman, Fadeel Sher Khan, Matthew C. Fox, Jason S. Reichenberg, Brett H. Keeling, Katherine R. Sebastian, Mia K. Markey, James W. Tunnell
Proc. SPIE 13838, Advanced Biomedical and Clinical Diagnostic and Surgical Guidance Systems XXIV (Photonics West), 2026
publisher

Mohs surgery is effective but slow and resource-intensive. We built a line-scanning confocal microscope that pairs spontaneous Raman spectroscopy with two fluorescence channels and uses superpixel binning to shorten Raman acquisition while keeping optical sectioning. We show slide-free, co-registered fluorescence and Raman imaging of unsectioned basal cell carcinoma specimens from Mohs patients.

MFSR-GAN: Multi-Frame Super-Resolution with Handheld Motion Modeling
Fadeel Sher Khan, Joshua Ebenezer, Hamid Sheikh, Seok-Jun Lee
CVPR Workshops (CVPRW), 2025 (NTIRE)
pdf arXiv publisher CVF

Existing multi-frame super-resolution datasets do not reproduce the noise and motion of real handheld bursts. We build a synthetic data engine that creates low- and high-resolution training pairs from multi-exposure static captures while keeping sensor noise and handheld motion, and we propose MFSR-GAN, a multi-scale RAW-to-RGB network that anchors on a base frame to reduce artifacts. Trained on this data, it gives sharper, more realistic reconstructions on both synthetic and real bursts.

Biophysically Interpretable Basal Cell Carcinoma Classification Using Raman Spectroscopy Transformer Model
Fadeel Sher Khan, Xu Feng, Matthew C. Fox, Jason S. Reichenberg, Fabiana C. P. Lopes, Katherine R. Sebastian, Mia K. Markey, James W. Tunnell
Proc. SPIE 13306, Advanced Biomedical and Clinical Diagnostic and Surgical Guidance Systems XXIII (Photonics West), 2025 (oral)
publisher

We classify basal cell carcinoma from Raman spectra with a transformer that tokenizes the spectrum, so its attention can be read against known tissue biochemistry. The wavenumber bands the model attends to match keratin, collagen, elastin, and lipid signatures, linking its predictions to the underlying biophysics.

Single Color Digital H&E Staining with In-and-Out Net
Mengkun Chen, Yen-Tung Liu, Fadeel Sher Khan, Matthew C. Fox, Jason S. Reichenberg, Fabiana C. P. S. Lopes, Katherine R. Sebastian, Mia K. Markey, James W. Tunnell
Computerized Medical Imaging and Graphics, 118: 102468, 2024
pdf arXiv publisher PubMed

In-and-Out Net is a GAN that converts reflectance confocal microscopy images of skin into virtual H&E. Aluminum chloride preprocessing boosts nuclear contrast, and training labels come from two-channel fluorescence images of the same tissue, which gives pixel-matched ground truth without image registration. The paper covers the training strategy, comparisons with prior methods, and an ablation study.
